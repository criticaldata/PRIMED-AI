\begin{abstract}
Echocardiography is the clinical reference for left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) but
requires a sonographer, a cart, and time; the electrocardiogram (ECG) is ubiquitous, cheap,
and acquired everywhere from the emergency department to the rural clinic. A multimodal model
trained on paired echo and ECG can silently assume both modalities are present at inference---an
assumption that breaks exactly where deployment is hardest. We report a work-in-progress study
designed around the deployment question rather than a benchmark delta: \emph{if a fused
echo\,+\,ECG model is trained on both modalities but only the ECG is available at inference, how
much accuracy is lost, and does the model degrade gracefully or fail silently?} We describe a
frozen-embedding pipeline pairing a video JEPA echo encoder~\citep{echojepa} with a
wav2vec2-style ECG foundation model~\citep{ecgfm} over MIMIC-IV~\citep{mimiciv}, a
missing-modality evaluation protocol, and a post-hoc fairness audit across sex, age, and race.
Consistent with this venue's welcome of work-in-progress and lessons-learned reports, we present
the released pipeline and protocols as the primary contribution and give a candid account of the
data-access and model-weight barriers that bounded full empirical results---barriers we argue are
themselves a deployment finding for cardiac foundation models.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The dominant evaluation of medical AI asks whether a model is \emph{accurate}; deployment asks
whether it is \emph{safe}, \emph{equitable}, and works \emph{where it is actually used}. These
diverge for multimodal models. Fusing echo and ECG can improve LVEF estimation when both modalities
are present, yet deployment is asymmetric: the ECG is acquired almost everywhere, while echo needs
equipment and expertise concentrated in well-resourced settings. A fused model that leans on the
echo branch may look strong in evaluation yet fail precisely in the low-resource settings that most
need a cheap screen. This report takes that asymmetry as its object: rather than advance
fusion-beats-unimodal, we study (i)~a \textbf{missing-modality robustness} protocol measuring
degradation when echo is withheld at inference, and (ii)~a \textbf{fairness audit} of EF error
across demographic strata---both deployment-readiness checks. Throughout we use \emph{frozen}
embeddings (no fine-tuning): cheap, reproducible, and a realistic stand-in for how a small team
would build such a system.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
  \item A deployment-framed problem statement for multimodal cardiac estimation under
        \emph{inference-time modality availability}, with the ECG-only regime as the case of interest.
  \item A frozen-embedding echo\,+\,ECG pipeline over MIMIC-IV with a missing-modality protocol and
        post-hoc fairness audit, released as open, unit-tested code.
  \item A candid account of the data-access, weight, and compute barriers to deployable cardiac
        foundation-model systems---offered as a finding, not an aside.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related}
Foundation models exist for both modalities: a video Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for
echocardiography~\citep{echojepa} and a wav2vec2-style self-supervised model for the 12-lead
ECG~\citep{ecgfm}. The closest prior work, EchoingECG~\citep{echoingecg}, is a probabilistic
student--teacher model that \emph{distills} echocardiographic knowledge into ECG representations
(probabilistic cross-modal embeddings with an echo--language teacher) so cardiac function can be
predicted from the ECG alone, with uncertainty estimates, in zero-/few-shot and fine-tuned settings.
Our study differs in kind, on three axes that matter for deployment rather than representation
quality: (i)~both backbones stay \emph{frozen} and we learn only lightweight probes---no distillation,
no fine-tuning---measuring what the released representations already encode; (ii)~we \emph{retain and
fuse} both modalities and ask what happens when one is \emph{missing at inference}, rather than
compressing echo into an ECG-only model; and (iii)~inference-time degradation and a demographic
fairness audit are our primary results. EchoingECG asks how to build a better ECG model using echo
at training time; we ask whether a deployed echo+ECG model fails safely when the echo is gone.

\section{Data and Cohort Design}
\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Sources.} MIMIC-IV-Echo (v0.1; echo DICOM studies and structured LVEF
labels)~\citep{mimicivecho}, MIMIC-IV-ECG (v1.0; 12-lead waveforms)~\citep{mimicivecg}, and
MIMIC-IV (v3.1; demographics)~\citep{mimiciv}. All three require PhysioNet credentialing and
signed Data Use Agreements. Cohort construction runs server-side on BigQuery so the full
$\sim$525K-study echo corpus is never materialized locally.

\paragraph{Pairing protocol.} For each echo study with a structured LVEF label, we match the
nearest ECG on the same \texttt{subject\_id} within a \mbox{24--48\,h} window; multi-match cases
resolve to the nearest timestamp. We model continuous LVEF and the clinically meaningful
\mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} (HFrEF) gate.

\paragraph{Splitting.} We partition by \texttt{subject\_id} (70/10/20 train/val/test) with an
explicit zero-leakage check, so no patient appears in more than one split.

\paragraph{Caveat.} MIMIC is single-center; estimates may not transfer across institutions, and we
note known gender-curation limitations in the demographic fields, relevant to the fairness audit
(\S\ref{sec:method}).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Frozen encoders.} EchoJEPA-L (ViT-L; 1024-dim, spatial-structure-preserving echo-video
embeddings)~\citep{echojepa} and ECG-FM (wav2vec2-style; token/sequence embeddings over the
12-lead waveform)~\citep{ecgfm}. Both run in inference mode only; gradients never flow into the
backbones. Embeddings are extracted once over the paired cohort and cached to disk, so probe
training is reproducible and completes in minutes.

\paragraph{Probes.} (a)~\emph{ECG-only}---pooled ECG-FM embedding $\rightarrow$ linear/MLP head.
(b)~\emph{Echo-only}---\textbf{attentive} pooling over EchoJEPA tokens $\rightarrow$ head (a linear
probe on the raw high-dimensional embedding is expected to underperform). (c)~\emph{Concat-MLP}---pooled
echo $\oplus$ pooled ECG $\rightarrow$ MLP (quick fused baseline). (d)~\emph{Cross-attention
fusion}---the headline model used for the missing-modality analysis. All probes predict continuous
LVEF; the \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} gate is derived from the continuous output. Primary metrics:
\textbf{MAE} (regression) and \textbf{EF\,$\leq$\,40\% AUROC} (gate). For context, the source
models report EchoJEPA $\approx 5.97$ MAE and ECG-FM $\approx 0.929$ AUROC for the
\mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} gate.

\paragraph{Missing-modality protocol (centerpiece).} Train the fused probe on full data; evaluate
the \emph{same deployed model} on the held-out test split under three conditions---\textbf{full}
(echo+ECG), \textbf{echo-dropped} (mask the echo branch: the ECG-only deployment, our case of
interest), and \textbf{ECG-dropped} (echo-only deployment)---by masking a branch at inference
rather than retraining a unimodal model. Report MAE and AUROC per condition and plot the
degradation curve (graceful vs.\ silent failure).

\paragraph{Fairness protocol.} Post-hoc, no extra training: stratify test predictions by sex, age
band, and race; report per-stratum MAE and \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\% AUROC}; flag small strata and the
MIMIC gender-curation caveat.

\section{Status and Results}
\label{sec:status}
We offer the implementation and protocols as the primary deliverable. \textbf{Implemented and
unit-tested:} frozen EchoJEPA-L and ECG-FM wrappers; the BigQuery cohort builder (pairing, LVEF
join, demographics, subject-level split, \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} prevalence, CONSORT flow export);
an on-disk embedding cache; ECG-only, echo-only attentive, concat-MLP, and cross-attention probes;
and the missing-modality and fairness evaluators. \textbf{Empirical results are deferred:} the
cohort build needs credentialed BigQuery access and extraction needs reserved GPU time, neither of
which cleared in the sprint window (\S\ref{sec:lessons}). Rather than report under-validated numbers
we release the code and protocols, so the degradation curve and fairness tables are a matter of
running, not building.

\section{Lessons Learned: the Access Path \emph{Is} a Deployment Finding}
\label{sec:lessons}
The gap between a code-complete pipeline and a fully-evaluated one was, in this sprint, almost
entirely an \emph{access} gap---and that gap is itself informative for anyone trying to deploy
cardiac foundation models.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{Credentialing latency across a team.} Every contributor who touches data needs
        PhysioNet credentialing, three signed DUAs, and per-dataset access requests. This is serial
        and slow, and it gates the entire data stage for the whole team, not one person.
  \item \textbf{The headline echo weights are not openly available.} The strongest echo encoder's
        weights were pending public release and had to be obtained privately. A pipeline whose
        central modality depends on a private weight transfer is, by definition, not yet
        deployable---and not independently reproducible by reviewers.
  \item \textbf{Compute reservation is a real-time dependency.} Embedding extraction needs a
        reserved GPU; the reservation, not the compute, sat on the critical path.
  \item \textbf{Pairing tooling had to be built from scratch.} There is no off-the-shelf
        echo$\leftrightarrow$ECG temporal join; the windowing, multi-match resolution, and
        leakage-safe splitting were all bespoke.
  \item \textbf{The deployment asymmetry mirrors the access asymmetry.} The modality that is hard
        to \emph{acquire at inference} (echo) is also the one that was hard to \emph{access for
        development} (private weights, DICOM volume). The cheap, ubiquitous modality (ECG) was the
        easy one on both axes. Our thesis, restated as a lived constraint: building for the
        data-rich setting is easy; the deployment-relevant, ECG-only setting is where both the
        engineering and the science get hard.
\end{itemize}

\noindent We argue these are not incidental sprint hiccups but structural features of the path from
a published cardiac foundation model to a deployable screening tool, and that reporting them is
squarely within this venue's remit.

\section{Discussion and Future Work}
\label{sec:discussion}
The deployment question reframes a multimodal model's value: not how much fusion adds when both
modalities are present, but how safely it behaves when the expensive one is absent---a degradation
curve and a graceful-vs-silent-failure test, with a fairness audit asking whether any degradation is
borne unequally. Next steps: run the missing-modality and fairness measurements once access clears;
extend to valvular hemodynamics and HFpEF phenotyping (both blocked by label availability); add
\mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} calibration; and pursue external multi-center validation. All code, configs,
and protocols are released, so the empirical results require only credentialed data access and a
single GPU to reproduce.
