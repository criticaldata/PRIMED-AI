\title{Loud or Silent? A Reusable Framework for Per-Modality\\
Failure Analysis in Multimodal Clinical AI}

\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Multimodal clinical models are usually judged on accuracy with every modality present, but
deployment removes modalities---an echocardiogram is often unavailable where an ECG is
routine---and then the useful questions are not just \emph{how much} accuracy is lost but
\emph{which} modality was responsible and whether the model fails \emph{loudly} or
\emph{silently}. Models are replaced often; the evaluation that answers these questions is reused.
We present a model-agnostic \textbf{modality-failure framework}: given $N$ modality embeddings,
any mask-aware probe, and labels, it returns a per-example failure taxonomy, a per-modality
complementarity matrix that attributes error to modalities, and a loud-vs-silent dropout profile
that separates monitorable failures from ones that pass unflagged far from the decision boundary, using only deployment-observable
signals. We release it as a small, unit-tested harness and validate it on synthetic data with
planted structure---across seeds it recovers the planted modality dominance and complementary
subset, reports per-modality loud-vs-silent rates, and scales to a three-modality complementarity
matrix. We instantiate it on \emph{frozen} EchoJEPA and ECG
embeddings~\citep{echojepa,vjepa2,hubertecg} for LVEF and the \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} HFrEF gate over
MIMIC-IV~\citep{mimiciv,johnson2023mimiciv}, where on a single real held-out cohort (\mbox{$n=245$}) dropping echo nearly
doubles error; per-example attribution and loud-vs-silent rates on \emph{real} data await
per-example predictions held back by the data-access barriers we document---themselves a deployment
finding for cardiac foundation models.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Foundation models for medical imaging and signals are replaced every few months; the evaluation
that exposes \emph{how} they fail is what compounds and gets cited. Yet multimodal clinical models
are still judged mostly on accuracy with every modality present. Deployment is not so generous: a
fused echo\,+\,ECG model may be trained on both, but at inference the ECG is acquired almost
everywhere while echo needs equipment and expertise concentrated in well-resourced settings. When a
modality goes missing, three questions matter that an accuracy number cannot answer---\emph{which}
modality was responsible for the error, whether the model fails \emph{loudly} (its output signals
doubt and a monitor can catch it) or \emph{silently} (far from the decision boundary yet wrong, so it passes unflagged), and how failures
distribute across patients.

We therefore argue for a \emph{failure-analysis framework} over yet another fusion model, and
provide one (Table~\ref{tab:pillars}). It is model-agnostic: given $N$ modality embeddings, any
mask-aware probe, and labels, it returns a per-example failure taxonomy, a per-modality
complementarity matrix, and a loud-vs-silent dropout profile, so it transfers across model
generations. We instantiate it on \emph{frozen} EchoJEPA and ECG embeddings (no fine-tuning---a
realistic stand-in for how a small team builds such a system) for LVEF regression and the
\mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} HFrEF gate over MIMIC-IV, validate that it recovers planted structure on
synthetic data, and demonstrate it on a real paired cohort. Scope is deliberately narrow
(LVEF / \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} only; valvular and HFpEF deferred for label reasons).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A model-agnostic modality-failure framework} with formal definitions for a
        per-example failure taxonomy, per-modality complementarity/attribution, and a
        loud-vs-silent dropout profile that uses only deployment-observable signals.
  \item \textbf{A small reusable harness} implementing it for any $N$ modalities and any
        mask-aware probe, validated on synthetic data with planted ground truth (across 12 seeds it
        recovers the planted dominance and complementary subset, and it scales to an $N$-modality
        complementarity matrix).
  \item \textbf{An instantiation} on frozen echo\,+\,ECG embeddings for LVEF/HFrEF over MIMIC-IV,
        with a real-data degradation demonstration and a candid account of the data-access barriers
        that bound full clinical results---themselves a deployment finding.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related}
Foundation models exist for both modalities: EchoJEPA adapts the V-JEPA2 latent-predictive
video architecture~\citep{vjepa2} to echocardiography~\citep{echojepa}, and HuBERT-ECG~\citep{hubertecg}
is a self-supervised model for the 12-lead ECG (HuBERT/wav2vec2 family~\citep{wav2vec2}). EchoingECG~\citep{echoingecg} distills echocardiographic knowledge into ECG
representations (probabilistic cross-modal embeddings with an echo--language teacher) so cardiac
function can be predicted from the ECG alone, with uncertainty, in zero-/few-shot and fine-tuned
settings. Our study differs on three deployment-relevant axes: (i)~both backbones stay
\emph{frozen} and we learn only lightweight probes---no distillation, no fine-tuning; (ii)~we
\emph{retain and fuse} both modalities and ask what happens when one is \emph{missing at
inference}, rather than compressing echo into an ECG-only model; and (iii)~inference-time
degradation and a demographic fairness audit are primary results. EchoingECG asks how to build a
better ECG model using echo at training time; we ask, model-agnostically, how a deployed
multimodal model fails when a modality is gone. Missing-modality robustness is typically reported
as a single aggregate accuracy drop~\citep{smil2021,mmrobust2022}; we instead attribute failures per modality and separate loud
from silent degradation, packaged as a reusable instrument rather than a one-off ablation.

\section{Data and Cohort Design}
\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Sources.} MIMIC-IV-Echo (v0.1; echo DICOM studies and structured LVEF
labels)~\citep{mimicivecho}, MIMIC-IV-ECG (v1.0; 12-lead waveforms)~\citep{mimicivecg}, and
MIMIC-IV (v3.1; demographics)~\citep{mimiciv,johnson2023mimiciv}. All three require
PhysioNet credentialing~\citep{goldberger2000} and signed Data Use Agreements. Cohort
construction runs server-side on BigQuery~\citep{mimiccloud} so the full $\sim$525K-study echo
corpus~\citep{mimicivecho} is never materialized locally.

\paragraph{LVEF label hierarchy.} LVEF is not stored in a single column: lab-system transitions
and rest/stress protocols yield several structured measurement identifiers. We take the first
available resting point estimate in priority order---\texttt{lvef} $\rightarrow$
\texttt{biplane\_lvef} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{rest\_lvef} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{rest\_biplane\_lvef}
$\rightarrow$ \texttt{lvef\_3d}, with range upper-bounds last---and record which field was used
(\texttt{lvef\_measurement}) for auditability. \texttt{stress\_lvef} is excluded (different
physiological state). We derive continuous LVEF and \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} (\texttt{ef\_le\_40}).

\paragraph{Pairing and splits.} For each labeled echo study we match the nearest ECG on the same
\texttt{subject\_id}. Default: symmetric $\pm$24\,h window; configurable asymmetric windows
(e.g.\ allow an ECG up to 30 days \emph{before} but only 24\,h after the echo) to mirror a
screen-first deployment story; alternative \emph{admission} pairing is encounter-aligned and
robust to documented ECG clock skew. Multi-match cases resolve to the nearest timestamp with
deterministic tie-breaking. Splits partition by \texttt{subject\_id} (70/10/20) with an explicit
zero-leakage check. Demographics (sex, anchor age, race, derived age bands) join for the
fairness audit; coverage and MIMIC gender-curation limitations are logged.

\section{Frozen-Embedding Pipeline}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Frozen encoders.} EchoJEPA-L (ViT-L; 1024-dim spatial echo-video
tokens)~\citep{echojepa} and HuBERT-ECG (12-lead ECG
tokens)~\citep{hubertecg}. Both run in inference mode only. Embeddings are written to an on-disk
cache: one \texttt{.npy} array per study/record ID, an append-only JSONL manifest, and an
\texttt{encoder\_version} tag per write so vectors remain traceable to the checkpoint that
produced them. Atomic writes and resume support let long extraction jobs survive interruption.

\paragraph{Probes.} Table~\ref{tab:probes} summarizes the four heads. Echo-only uses
\textbf{attentive} pooling (a linear probe on raw high-dimensional EchoJEPA tokens is expected
to underperform). Cross-attention fusion is the headline model for missing-modality analysis:
bidirectional multi-head attention between echo and ECG token sequences, followed by an MLP
regression head. All probes predict continuous LVEF; the \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} gate is derived
from predictions. Metrics: \textbf{MAE} and \textbf{EF\,$\leq$\,40\% AUROC} (context from source
models, on different fine-tuned cohorts not comparable to our frozen split: EchoJEPA $\approx 5.97$ MAE and a reported ECG model $\approx 0.93$ AUROC).

\paragraph{Missing-modality protocol (centerpiece).} Train the cross-attention probe on full
(echo+ECG) data. Evaluate the \emph{same deployed checkpoint} on the held-out test split under
three conditions by zeroing a branch at inference---\textbf{full}, \textbf{echo-dropped}
(ECG-only deployment), and \textbf{ECG-dropped} (echo-only)---rather than retraining unimodal
models. Report MAE and AUROC per condition with bootstrap confidence intervals when per-example predictions are available; plot the
degradation curve. This directly tests graceful vs.\ silent failure when the expensive modality
is absent.

\paragraph{Fairness protocol.} Post-hoc on test predictions: stratify by sex, age band, and
race; report per-stratum MAE and \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\% AUROC}; flag strata below a minimum
count. No extra training. MIMIC gender-curation limitations are documented alongside coverage
statistics.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Hydra configuration groups (\texttt{encoder/}, \texttt{cohort/},
\texttt{probe/}, \texttt{eval/}), fixed random seeds, and run manifests recording git SHA and
resolved config accompany each artifact under \texttt{logs/runs/}.

\section{A Framework for Modality-Failure Analysis}
\label{sec:framework}
We formalize a model-agnostic analysis that assumes only a \emph{mask-aware} predictor: given
modalities $\mathcal{M}$, a held-out set with regression target $y$ (LVEF) and clinical gate
$g=\mathbb{1}[y\le\tau]$ ($\tau{=}40$), and a function $f(S)$ returning a prediction per example
when only the subset $S\subseteq\mathcal{M}$ is present (others masked at inference), it reports
three views. Nothing else about $f$ is assumed, so it applies to any probe, fusion architecture, or
backbone.

\paragraph{(i) Failure taxonomy.} Each example, under each condition, is labelled \emph{correct}
($g$ right, $|f-y|\le\delta$), \emph{imprecise} ($g$ right, $|f-y|>\delta$), or \emph{critical}
($g$ wrong---an HFrEF case missed or false-alarmed). Critical errors, not raw MAE, are the
clinically actionable failures; a large regression error that does not flip the gate stays
\emph{imprecise} by design.

\paragraph{(ii) Complementarity and attribution.} Each modality's leave-one-out value is
$\Delta_m{=}\mathrm{MAE}(f(\mathcal{M}{\setminus}\{m\}))-\mathrm{MAE}(f(\mathcal{M}))$, and per
example we attribute the ``win'' to the modality whose removal hurts that example most. Solo and
pairwise errors form a \emph{complementarity matrix} separating \emph{redundant} modalities (either
alone suffices) from \emph{complementary} ones (both needed). Views stratify by patient attributes
(sex, age, race): a modality that wins overall may not win within a subgroup, tying attribution to
equity.

\paragraph{(iii) Loud-vs-silent dropout profile.} When $m$ is missing, an example that was
gate-correct with $\mathcal{M}$ but is now gate-wrong is an \emph{induced critical failure}. We split
these by a deployment-observable signal---the prediction's margin from the decision boundary: a
failure is \emph{silent} if the wrong prediction sits far on the wrong side ($|f-\tau|\ge\kappa$, so
it looks unremarkable and a margin monitor would not flag it) and \emph{loud} if it lands near the
threshold ($|f-\tau|<\kappa$, where the output invites a second read). Margin is a \emph{proxy for
monitorability}, not calibrated confidence---a probabilistic head (cf.\ EchoingECG) would sharpen
it---and uses only the deployed prediction, not the unavailable full-modality counterfactual. A high
silent rate is the dangerous regime: wrong, far from the cut, and unflagged.

\paragraph{Reusable harness.} The three views ship as a small library,
\mbox{\texttt{analyze(embeddings, predict\_fn, labels)}} $\rightarrow$
\{\texttt{taxonomy}, \texttt{complementarity}, \texttt{dropout}\}, taking $N$ modality embeddings,
any mask-aware \texttt{predict\_fn}, and labels (Listing~\ref{lst:api}). It is released and
unit-tested, and is the artifact we argue outlives any particular fusion model.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,caption={Using the harness: any $N$ modalities, any mask-aware probe.},label=lst:api]
from primed_ai.failure import analyze_modality_failure

report = analyze_modality_failure(
    embeddings={'echo': echo_emb, 'ecg': ecg_emb},  # any N modalities
    lvef=y, ef_le_40=gate,                           # regression target + clinical gate
    predict_fn=probe.masked_predict,                 # subset -> predictions (others masked)
    groups={'sex': sex, 'age_band': age},            # optional: per-stratum attribution
)
report.complementarity['marginal_value']    # e.g. {'echo': 5.5, 'ecg': 1.8}
report.dropout['drop_echo']['silent_rate']  # loud vs. silent when echo is missing
report.conditions['drop_echo']['taxonomy']  # correct / imprecise / critical counts
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Lessons Learned: the Access Path \emph{Is} a Deployment Finding}
\label{sec:lessons}
The gap between a code-complete pipeline and a fully-evaluated one was almost
entirely an \emph{access} gap:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Credentialing latency across a team.} Every contributor needs PhysioNet
        credentialing, three DUAs, and per-dataset BigQuery requests---serial, team-wide gates.
  \item \textbf{Echo weights are not openly available.} The strongest echo encoder required a
        private checkpoint transfer; a pipeline whose central modality depends on that is not
        yet independently reproducible.
  \item \textbf{Compute reservation, not FLOPs, was the bottleneck.} Embedding extraction needs
        a reserved GPU slot on a shared cluster.
  \item \textbf{Pairing tooling had to be built from scratch.} No off-the-shelf echo$\leftrightarrow$ECG
        temporal join exists; windowing, tie-breaking, and leakage-safe splits were bespoke.
  \item \textbf{Deployment asymmetry mirrors access asymmetry.} The modality hard to acquire at
        inference (echo) was also hard to access for development (private weights, DICOM volume).
        The cheap, ubiquitous modality (ECG) was easy on both axes---restating our thesis as a
        lived constraint.
\end{itemize}
We argue these are structural features of the path from a published cardiac foundation model to
a deployable screening tool.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
Several constraints bound our conclusions. \textbf{Single-center data:} MIMIC is one US academic
center, so degradation and fairness patterns may not transfer across institutions or acquisition
hardware---an external-validation gap we cannot close here. \textbf{Frozen-embedding ceiling:} by
design we never fine-tune the backbones, so absolute accuracy is a lower bound on what the
representations could achieve; our claims concern \emph{relative} degradation under missing
modalities, not state-of-the-art LVEF estimation. \textbf{Label and cohort scope:} we target
structured LVEF and the \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} gate only, and \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} prevalence in
a paired echo+ECG cohort may be modest, widening AUROC confidence intervals. \textbf{Echo-branch
reproducibility:} the strongest echo encoder relies on a privately shared checkpoint; until public
release, independent reproduction of the echo arm is limited. \textbf{Margin is not calibrated
confidence:} the loud/silent split uses distance from the \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} threshold as a
proxy for monitorability, not a probabilistic confidence; a calibrated head would sharpen it.
\textbf{Two-modality attribution is partly degenerate:} with $N{=}2$ the leave-one-out winner
equals the surviving-modality solo, so per-example attribution is most informative for $N{\ge}3$.
\textbf{Fairness caveats:} a demographic audit inherits MIMIC's known gender- and race-curation
limitations, small per-stratum samples (wide or undefined AUROC), and post-hoc confounding by
differing disease prevalence and presentation across groups---observed gaps may reflect the data,
not only the model.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The deployment question reframes multimodal value: not how much fusion adds when both modalities
are present, but \emph{which} modality is responsible and whether the model fails loudly or
silently when one is gone. Because the framework assumes only a mask-aware predictor, it transfers
to the next echo or ECG backbone without change---the evaluation outlives the model, which is the
case we make for shipping an instrument rather than a checkpoint. Our synthetic study shows
why the two axes must be \emph{measured}, not assumed: echo-dominance was robust across seeds, yet
the loud-vs-silent rates of the two modalities were comparable---value and detectability need not
coincide. Next steps: run the per-example attribution and loud-vs-silent
profile on real predictions; add \mbox{EF\,$\leq$\,40\%} calibration; extend to more modalities and
to valvular/HFpEF tasks (label blockers); and pursue external validation. All code, configs, and the
harness are released.
